The rope

A rope made of loops.

RunLock is a braided polyester rope whose two halves are braided together at regular intervals and run free in between. Every free stretch is a loop. That is the whole idea, and everything the rope can do follows from it.

How it is made →Specifications
How it is made

Two sides. Joined every few centimetres.

The rope is braided from polyester threads in two groups, one for each side. The size number is the number of threads in each group: a No 4 has 4 + 4 threads, a No 20 has 20 + 20. Every thread is three yarns twisted together. On the braiding machine the two groups are braided through each other for a short stretch, then run apart, then braided together again. Each time they meet, the groups swap sides. The free stretch is the loop. The braided stretch is the join that carries the load.

8–40Threads

The size number is the thread count per side: No 4 is 4 + 4 threads, No 20 is 20 + 20. Each thread is three twisted yarns.

40–65 mmLoop length

Shorter loops on the thin sizes, longer on the thick. The loop is where the rope goes through itself.

100 %Strength in every loop

There is no knot to weaken the rope. The braided join carries the full breaking strength, from the first loop to the last.

Why a loop holds

No knot. Just the rope through itself.

Every RunLock lock is the same move: the rope, or a fold of it, is pushed through one of its own loops. When the rope is loaded, the loop is pulled tight around the part that passes through it. The harder you pull, the harder it grips. Slack it off and the grip opens again, because nothing has been tied.

That is why the rope does not lose strength at the lock. A knot bends a rope sharply around itself, and the fibres on the outside of the bend carry far more than the fibres on the inside. RunLock loads the braided join, which is made to carry the load straight through. The four locks differ only in how the rope is led through the loop: sliding (the running loop), fixed (the fixed loop), locked but quick to release (the quick loop) or wrapped around an anchor (the securing lock).

Because the loops sit every few centimetres, every lock is adjustable in steps of one loop. Move the end one loop forward and the rope is one loop shorter. There is nothing to retie.

Joining two ropes

Two RunLocks join loop to loop: pass the end of one through a loop of the other, then back through its own loop. The join holds like the rope itself and comes apart with one pull when the load is off. Join two No 16 to pull with more force, or a No 8 to a No 4 to reach a little further.

Strength and load

Tested at SP, rated with a margin.

Every size has been pull-tested at SP, the Swedish national testing institute: a new rope, loaded in a straight pull until it breaks. The No 20 held 843 kg before it broke. We rate it at 700 kg, and every size gets its rating the same way, well below the force it actually held.

The rating is the strength of the rope, not the load to work it at every day. Tying down and pulling means jerks, wet rope and corners, so pick a size with strength to spare. For towing, that is the No 20 and a gentle start: the stretch in the rope smooths the pull out.

RunLock is a rope for pulling, tying down, hanging and leading. It is not a lifting sling or a climbing rope, and it is not certified for lifting loads over people.

Stretch

Every size stretches by a fifth to a quarter of its length before it breaks, 21 to 25 % in the SP test, and springs back when the load is off. In a towing line that stretch is what turns a jerk into a pull: the rope takes up the first metre of movement gently instead of passing the shock straight into the two cars. In a tie-down it is what keeps the load tight: the rope is slightly stretched when you lock it, and it keeps pulling as the load settles.

Material

Polyester, because it behaves.

RunLock is 100 % polyester, chosen for how little it changes with weather, water and time.

Water

Polyester takes up almost no water. The rope does not swell, does not get heavy and does not stiffen when wet, and it keeps its strength wet or dry. It does not float.

Sun and cold

Good resistance to UV light, so the rope can live outdoors. The fibre keeps its flexibility in the cold, and a frozen RunLock still releases with one pull.

Oil, fuel and salt

Strong resistance to petroleum products, salt water and most everyday chemicals. Hang it in the garage, keep it in the boat.

Heat

Polyester melts at roughly 250 °C. That is what lets you seal a cut end with a lighter, and why the rope should be kept away from exhausts and open fire.

Wear

The braid is tight and smooth, so the rope runs through its own loops without fraying. Sharp edges are the enemy of any rope: pad them, or take the load on a fixed loop instead of a sliding one.

Soft on things

No hooks, no chains, no ratchets. The rope will not scratch paint, dent a canoe or rattle in the forest, and it is comfortable to pull by hand.

Care

Cut it, wash it, keep it.

Cutting to length

Cut in the middle of a loop, across both strands. Then melt each cut end briefly with a lighter and press the two strands together, so the end is sealed in one piece and the last join holds. A cut length is a complete tool: no eye to splice, no hardware to fit.

Washing

Machine wash at 40 °C, without fabric softener. Let it dry hanging. Mud, salt and resin all come out, and the colours stay.

Inspection

Before a heavy pull, run the rope through your hands. Look for cut strands, a join that has come open, melted or glazed patches and loops that have stretched out of shape. A rope that has taken a real shock load or shows damage is retired to lighter work.

Storage

Coil it loosely and hang it dry. The rope tolerates sun, but a rope kept in the dark lasts longer than one left on the dashboard for years. Keep it away from sharp tools in the toolbox.

Tested and trusted

From the forests of Dalsland.

RunLock was born in 1983, when the founder of Texsolv needed a flexible, reliable rope for everything from pulling game out of the forest to hanging tools in the workshop. He took the loop idea from the Texsolv heddle cord that the factory had made for hand looms since 1977, and gave it a stronger braid and built-in stretch.

Since then Texsolv has braided every metre in Sweden, today at Vitlanda in Tösse. The breaking strengths were tested by SP, now RISE, the Swedish research institute, and RunLock has been supplied to armed forces, police and professionals as well as to hunters, boat owners, farmers and dog people around the world.

Test report

Tensile test at SP, Borås, January 2016

Six new ropes, 2 m long, one of each size, were pulled to breaking in a tensile testing machine at SP Structural and Solid Mechanics in Borås (now part of RISE). Each rope was fixed with 16.4 mm shackles, pre-loaded to 0.1 kN, and then loaded at a constant 50 mm/min until it broke. Every rope broke at the loop, not in the braid. Measurement uncertainty for the breaking force is below 1 %.
